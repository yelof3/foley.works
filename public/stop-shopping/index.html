Stop Shopping

Stop shopping. Avoid the Shopocalypse. Changealujah!

October 31, 2026

  • capitalism
  • consumerism
  • blog

I learned of Reverend Billy and the Church of Stop Shopping on my second trip to Burning Man (wow, was it really 2003?), and I instantly knew that this was the only congregation for me.

Here’s a sample of some of their work.

Fun side note: In fetching this embed, I realized that this particular album is co-produced, mixed, and mastered by Danny Blume, who I first learned about from Liminal, which I learned about via DJ Olive (still have a Liminal CD stuck in my Numark Mixdeck, RIP). Always great to see heavies working with heavies.

I wondered by The church’s message resonated with me so strongly compared to my friends, and I think it mostly had to do with my Mom.

Upbringing

I was raised in a very Irish Catholic home. Like, go to every mass during Holy Week type of Irish Catholics. She made my and my siblings pray in front of the manager before we were able to open any presents. Along with that, my mom detested the consumerism that came along with Christmas–to her, all the presents nonsense got in the way of the real meaning of the occasion, and over the years, that definitely trickled down to me.

Perhaps it was from her being raised at just after the great depression, but along with her anti-consumerism, she also was someone who hated to waste things. In our family, we stopped using things because they were no longer useful, not becasue they fell out of style. That wasn’t the easiest growing up, but looking back, I’m very glad I was brought up that way.

The Uninportance of the Joneses

Other than when I was 8 and my parents didn’t let me get parachute pants like all my friends at the time, I’ve never really felt the urge to keep up with the Joneses. In fact, as I got a bit older, seeing the behavior was rather repulsing to me. The important things to me have always been family, friendship, love, trust, learning, fun, art, and so on. That’s what I care about: the good stuff that you can never put a price on. I couldn’t care any less about being seen somewhere, in some particular car, in some particular outfit, with some group of people. While it undoubtedly took a bit of time after the parachute pants fiasco to feel this way, nowadays I just don’t care much about how I appear to others.

Fashion (ugh)

Speaking of particular outfits - not caring about fashion makes it really easy to get a ton of miles and value out of items of clothes. Ollie holes in my Chucks, rips in my jeans, etc–easily patchable, or apparel wounds of glory. One might even call my anti-fashion stance and well-worn clothes as ‘punk’. While I’m spiritually a punk, I’ve never wrapped myself up in the fashion of punk. But you know what annoyed me more than anything? When retailers like Hot Topic started selling jeans with pre-made holes in them. How pathetic and backwards, on both sides of the coin. Or like when Nirvana made old flannel shirts popular: What they were doing was very much not keeping up with the Joneses, but what happened afterward was that a bunch of kids bought new, overpriced flannel shirts that hit the market as a result of Nirvana. When punk gets bottled up onto a Hot Topic shelf, it’s no longer punk, kids.

Environmentalism

Environmentalism is another big reason why the church’s message hit home so directly with me. In case you haven’t noticed, the world is burning. Managers of capitalists systems simply don’t care about the future of humanity or lots of other life on this planet, and are going to ride that inferno until the end. As consumers (or anti-consumers!), we do have at least some say in the trajectory of this seemingly unavoidable peril. While it’s true that the biggest changes that can be made to address this issue are in the realms of national law, and industrial regulation, there are things that us tiny people can do that can make an impact. Some examples:

  • rejection of fast fashion, mending
  • holiday present truces
  • using more public transportation
  • biycles 🚲
  • public transportation
  • political involvement
  • avoidance of disposable/single-use plastics
  • sourcing produce locally
  • cooking at home more
  • buying second hand

May we all stop shopping and avoid consumerism. Changealujah!
